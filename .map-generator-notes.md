## The thing that actually mattered: the trees were planted on the pavement

Following the corollary above — declare what the router cannot see — the first
step was to ask *what* it cannot see, per district, from the walker audit rather
than from `undeclared.mjs`. That turned out to matter, because
**`undeclared.mjs` cannot see this defect at all**: it skips instanced meshes
and anything over twelve metres across, and it samples centrelines. Neither can
`footway-audit`, which does not move by a single decimal for the change below.
Only the per-triangle walker audit sees it.

Every dominant site in the Old Quarter and on Sovereign Arc was `instanced`, at
a depth exactly equal to the walker's own radius — the walker's *centre* inside
the box, not its shoulder clipping an edge — 1.0 to 1.5 m tall, and reported as
`no-props`, meaning nothing declared stood anywhere near. `whatis.mjs` named
them: a two-child group of instanced meshes, 215 to 256 triangles, 0.6 to 0.9 m
across. Tree crowns.

The arithmetic is conclusive and needs no measurement to see once stated. A
local street is .92 wide, so its kerb is at .46; `KERB_TO_PAVEMENT` is .28 and
`STREET_PAVEMENT_HALF` is .09, so the walkable band runs from .65 to .83 out
from the centreline. `addCityEnvironment` plants its street trees at **.82**,
with a crown reaching about .31. The tree is not beside the pavement. It is
standing in the middle of it, at chest height, and every walker who uses that
street walks through the foliage from one end to the other.

The repair is that the tree moves, and it is the mirror image of every arm that
failed: no pavement is removed, nothing is declared, no route is constrained.
`cutFootwaysAroundSolids` could not have helped even if the crowns had been
declared — the band is .09 wide and the crown is .31, so the cut would have
deleted the pavement rather than narrowed it, which is the documented dead end.

### What each arm was

- **Authored-parcel siting.** The tier offices and the rival compounds are the
  only solids on the map never reconciled with the *pedestrian* network — the
  planned buildings and the loose props both are, via `buildingCorridors`, which
  has always included the footways. Their sites come from the route curve and a
  fixed table; the towns then lay a full grid over the same ground. So: put the
  footways into the main clearance field too and site those parcels against it
  before anything is built on them.
- **Civic fit.** `createCourthouse` is 5.2 x 3.5 at scale 1, and
  `addCircuitTown` drops it at a fixed .74 (seat) or .44 (village) on whatever
  block the lattice happened to produce, with the market cross and the two lamps
  at fixed fractions of that block. Nothing checked the block had room. Cut them
  to the plot instead, exactly as the Old Quarter courthouse already is.
- **Lattice anchoring.** `assembleAxis` drops the closer of any two lines within
  `minGap` in favour of the higher street class, and a town's own bounding lanes
  are `alley`, the lowest class there is. Fenwick — the county seat — loses its
  eastern back lane to a jittered local street 1.79 away and its southern one to
  a chain of three merges, and comes out **two blocks**, with a 1.89 x 3.05
  "market place" carrying a 3.85 x 2.59 courthouse. Anchoring the six lines that
  define a town fixes that.

### Why they failed: the map is held together by the zero-width ways

The reading that fits every arm, including this morning's:

A `half: 0` way is not merely a bad path. It is a **straitjacket**. A walker on
a way with no lateral freedom is pinned to the centreline, and the centrelines
are, by construction, the middle of the street. The Circuit's .0427 baseline —
four contact sites, all grazing, worst depth .06, all of them a cattle trough —
is *achieved by* the collapsed bands, not in spite of them.

`cutFootwaysAroundSolids` narrows a band only around **declared** solids. Most
of The Circuit is not declared: the farmsteads, the church, the field gates, the
cottage interiors and everything `bakeBatches` merges are invisible to it and
fully visible to the per-triangle audit. So the moment a band is allowed to
reopen — by shrinking the courthouse standing on it, by moving a compound off
it, by restoring the back lane it belonged to — the walkers it was holding on
the centreline spread out into geometry nobody ever declared. Arm C is the
cleanest demonstration: the *only* thing it does is make three set-pieces
smaller, and it takes the district from .0427 to .2396.

This is the same lesson the brief draws from attempt three, one level down.
Attempt three concentrated traffic by removing pavement; these arms diffuse it
by restoring pavement. Both make walkers-inside worse, because the pavement was
never the thing keeping people out of buildings — the *absence* of pavement was.

The corollary is the one useful piece of direction to come out of today:
**declaring solids improves the metric and freeing bands worsens it.** The
stalls commit is that corollary landed. The order of work for the next agent is
therefore inverted from the brief's: run `undeclared.mjs` per district, declare
what it finds, and only then fix the civic fit and the parcel siting. Fixing the
geometry first, on this map, is measurably harmful.

## Authoring the landmark sites from the street plan

The seventeen authored parcels — five headquarters and four rival compounds in
the Old Quarter, two and two on The Circuit, two and two on the Sovereign Arc —
were the only buildings in the game whose positions were written down rather
than planned. A table named a coordinate, the district then laid its streets,
and whichever of the two happened to be there won. Sixteen lost.

### What changed

`siteOnPlan` inverts the relationship. The authored position becomes a request;
the plan answers with the nearest ground it has actually left free. Three
details are what make it work where three previous escape passes did not:

1. **The declared footprint, oriented.** The same rectangle `createLevelBuilding`
   and `createRivalBuilding` mark solid, tested with `clearanceIntrusion`'s
   support-width path, so a site that clears at siting time cannot be found
   overlapping at routing time.
2. **`plannedGround`.** The terraces go into the same segment field as the
   pavements, taken from `buildingAudit`. Every earlier pass worked from
   `crowdObstacles`, which cannot see an instanced building, so it could move a
   compound out of a lane and into a cottage without noticing. A rectangle is
   carried as the corridor along its own longer axis with the shorter
   half-extent for a width — the stadium that contains it.
3. **An ordered sweep, not a gradient.** Two pavements meeting at a corner give
   a gradient a local minimum it cannot leave, which is exactly why the escape
   pass reported sixteen of seventeen parcels with no clear ground within 3.4 m.
   That was a false negative. It is anisotropic for a headquarters, which is set
   back from a named stretch of route: a deeper forecourt on the same street is
   cheap, a slide up the road is expensive (`lateralCost`).

### The line that mattered more than the siting

Moving a parcel silently stopped `clearAuthoredParcel` running where the table
had asked for it, leaving scenery standing that every previous build deleted —
226 extra solids on The Circuit. Clearing both the asked-for parcel and the
granted one is one line and it is the difference between a 30% regression and a
tenfold improvement. **A change that moves an object must also account for what
used to happen at the place it left.**

### Two blind spots, both silent, both found by looking rather than measuring

The first three contacts each reported `cleared` with `moved 0.00` and stood at
the exact centre of the district they belonged to — inside a tree apiece.

1. **`crowdObstacles` cannot see an instanced mesh.** Known for buildings, which
   is why `plannedGround` reads `buildingAudit`. Trees have the same problem and
   had no audit at all, so `recordTrees` now writes one the way
   `renderPlannedBuildings` writes its own.
2. **`prepareClearance` discards a corridor written as two identical points.**
   `if (length < 1e-4) continue`. A round obstacle expressed as a degenerate
   segment is dropped on the way into the field, the field builds without error,
   and every query against it answers "clear". `discCorridor` gives the segment
   a 2 mm length, which is the whole fix. Anything else adding a round keep-out
   to a clearance field should use it rather than rediscovering this.

### Street furniture: attempted, measured four to fourteen times worse, reverted

`furnitureOffPavement` moved every prop under a .62 footprint radius that was
not an enclosed mass and not a click target off the pedestrian ground, with the
tree pass's own limits — a 1.1 unit budget, eight passes, and nothing ever
deleted.

| arm | Old Quarter | The Circuit | Sovereign Arc |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| control | .0021 .0021 .0021 | .0086 .0086 .0086 | .0314 .0329 .0314 |
| furniture off pavement | .0131 .0043 .0043 | .0639 .1188 .0562 | .0136 .0314 .0219 |

The Circuit is seven to fourteen times worse and the Old Quarter twice as bad.
Reverted. This is the third time a pass that moves the small prop class has
measured worse than doing nothing, and it is now worth stating the likely
mechanism rather than only the result: a bench beside a path is furniture the
crowd steers around at a shoulder, and moving it off the paving puts it in the
open ground the crowd actually uses to pass one another. The tree pass works
because a crown at chest height over a .09 band leaves a walker no lateral
offset at all; a bench does. **Do not treat the two classes alike.** The named
sites — `nation-green-bench-1`, `nation-milestone-1-e`, `city-market-fountain` —
remain open, and the next attempt should move the *paving*, not the prop.
