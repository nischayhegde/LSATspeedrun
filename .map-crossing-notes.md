# Sovereign Arc: the remaining walker-versus-vehicle contacts

Worktree `/private/tmp/lsat-crossing`, branch `feat/crossing`, cut from
`integration/all-features` at `7977772`. Session ran 20:12 to 21:40.

**Nothing in `frontend/` was changed. There is nothing to revert.** The two
commits on this branch are both QA probes. What follows is a measurement
result, and its headline is that the premise this session was given is wrong.

## The premise, and why it does not survive measurement

The brief inherited this conclusion from `.map-generator-notes.md`:

> Sovereign Arc's remaining sites are all pavement at a crossing — `road@0,-19`,
> `road@0,15`, `road@9,9`, `road@0,14` — which is a crossing-timing question and
> a different defect.

That was inferred from **site coordinates**, and a coordinate cannot tell apart
the two things that produce one:

- a walker halfway across a road when a car arrives — a timing fault, fixable in
  the crossing rules, which is what this session was scoped to;
- a walker on a pavement that lies inside a carriageway or a tram alignment,
  who is not crossing anything and whose timing cannot be improved because no
  timing decision is being taken.

`tools/map-qa/crossing-attrib.mjs` (new, commit `c9df6be`, corrected in
`3b75c2c`) asks the walker instead of the map: at the moment of containment it
reads `walker.crossing`, `crossPhase`, `crossProgress`, the conflict list the
crossing was built with, and the edge the vehicle was actually on. Same
containment test as `collide.mjs` — shoulder disc against the hull's oriented
rectangle, no margin — and the same body radius, read off a live rig by the
same arithmetic.

### Sovereign Arc, 3600 frames, three replicates, every contact classified

| replicate | total hits | crossing, moving | crossing, at kerb | pavement, road | pavement, tram |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| r1 | 187 | 23 | 17 | 73 | 74 |
| r2 | 291 | 12 | 45 | 28 | 206 |
| r3 | 50 | 21 | 3 | 26 | 0 |
| **total** | **528** | **56** | **65** | **127** | **280** |

**Twenty-three per cent of the contacts involve a walker who is crossing at
all, and about half of that is a walker still standing at the kerb** — depths
of .004 to .066 against a .2835 body, which is a graze by a passing flank, not
a body in a vehicle. The largest single class, at 53%, is a walker on a
pavement being run down by a tram.

The flags rule out the failure modes the previous fixes addressed: `kerbside`
0, `noConflicts` 0, `pastEnd` 0 on every crossing-state hit across all three
runs. So the untimed-crossing fix and the hold-until-the-body-arrives fix are
both still doing their job — every crossing-state contact happened on a link
that *had* a resolved conflict list and was being timed against it.

## The instrument is even noisier than the brief says, and here is the spread

`collide.mjs`, `MAPS_FRAMES=3600`, Sovereign Arc, `inVehicle.bodyInsideFrames`,
three replicates on this stack, no change of any kind between them:

| r1 | r2 | r3 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **36** | **195** | **100** |

`pedestrianHits` on the same three runs: 136 / 561 / 62. The worst-site lists
have **no site in common** between replicates:

- r1 `road@-9,-11` 21, `road@6,-6` 13, `road@7,-6` 2
- r2 `transport@1,8` 93, `transport@0,8` 76, `transport@1,7` 35, `road@6,-5` 33
- r3 `road@3,10` 30, `road@4,10` 27, `road@-9,-11` 20, `road@6,-6` 13

This is consistent with the historical 51 / 66 / 141 / 141 / 187 / 227 and with
the 127 in the brief. **The figure is not a rate, it is a coincidence counter.**
One walker who happens to be standing on the tram alignment when the tram comes
round contributes ninety-odd frames on its own; the same scene with the crowd a
few centimetres out of phase contributes none. The mechanism is visible in the
early replicates I took at the wrong body radius, where five of seven runs
scored exactly zero and the other two scored 80 and 23.

**Consequence for anybody who follows.** To detect a change of the size on
offer here — say 127 down to 60 — against a spread of 36 to 195, you need
something like fifteen to twenty replicates per arm, not three. At roughly 25
seconds a run that is affordable; at three replicates it is not, and three
replicates is what everybody, including this session, has been budgeting. I
would not accept any result on this metric under ten replicates per arm.

The probe is reading the scene, not failing open: `walkerFrames` is 39578 to
39587 on every run, and `liveVehicleFrames` and `vehicles` (17) are stable, so
a zero is a real zero.

## The tram overlap: a hypothesis raised and then killed in the same hour

Since the dominant class is a walker on a pavement inside a tram's path, the
obvious next question is whether pavement is laid inside the tram alignment.
`planFootways` cuts pavements against carriageways only and says so — "a rail
line or a shipping lane crossing a pavement is not a kerb" — and the railway
right-of-way is cut separately by the scene.

`tools/map-qa/rail-overlap.mjs` (new) answers it as pure geometry, no frames,
so it is free of the burstiness above: for every footway vertex, is the walkable
band, `|centre| + halfWidth`, inside the transport's swept half-beam?

| district | ways | overlapping vertices | ways affected | body-inside-vehicle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sovereign Arc | 165 | 36 | 11 | 36 / 195 / 100 |
| Old Quarter | 804 | 43 | 31 | **0** |

**The Old Quarter has more of this defect than the Arc and scores zero.** So
pavement-inside-the-tram-path does not by itself produce contacts, and a pass
that cleaned it up could not be justified by these numbers. Whatever separates
the two districts is something else — tram frequency over the shared spans, or
which pavements the crowd actually populates — and I did not get to it.

**Caveat on this probe, stated because it changes what the number means.** It
counts vertices near the path and does not distinguish a pavement that *runs
along* the alignment from one that legitimately *crosses* it at a right angle;
a level crossing puts a vertex near the line too. The counts are therefore an
upper bound on the real defect, and the Arc's worst entries — way 67 at
`(0.74,7.39)`, `(1.15,7.38)`, `(2.43,7.23)`, and way 13 at `(-0.74,7.72)`, all
with the band .45 to .75 m inside the swept path — want eyes on them before
anybody acts. Note that those coordinates are exactly the `transport@1,7`,
`transport@0,8` and `transport@1,8` sites r2 named.

   minutes.
2. **Split the two districts' tram behaviour**, since geometry alone does not

   and the Old Quarter's does not, the fix is in the alignment, not in timing.
3. **Only then consider the crossing rules.** They are responsible for at most
   a quarter of this number and about an eighth of it at any depth worth

# Second half: the zeros are not zeros, and the noise has structure

Three things changed after the section above was written. Taken in the order
they matter.

## 1. The Circuit is not at zero, and neither district's zero was ever measured

The 0 / 0 / 127 everybody has been protecting is a single draw. I measured the
two "clean" districts myself, `collide.mjs`, 3600 frames, `bodyInsideFrames`:

| district | fresh-server replicates | earlier server |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Old Quarter `city` | **0, 0, 0** | 22 |
| The Circuit `nation` | **17, 0, 15** | 16 |
| Sovereign Arc `continent` | 156, 100, 138 | 36, 195, 100 |

**The Circuit scores non-zero in three of its four runs**, and it is not a
scatter: `road@4,-5` carries 17, 15 and 16 frames in the three runs that have
any, at depths .08 to .09. The same site, the same magnitude, across a server
restart. That is a reproducible defect sitting under a banked zero.

The Old Quarter is the one district that looks genuinely clean — three fresh
zeros — but its 22 frames at `road@-10,13` on the earlier server means "clean"
here means "usually zero", not "zero".

**This reframes the whole job.** The brief's instruction was to fix the Arc
without regressing two districts at zero. One of those two is not at zero, and
nobody knew because nobody had taken a second replicate. A change that took the
Arc from 130 to 100 and The Circuit from 16 to 0 would have read, on single
runs, as a regression.

## 2. Hot-reload contamination is real elsewhere but is not what I measured

Tested directly, as asked. Vite killed, restarted, three `collide.mjs`
replicates on the Arc taken back to back with no edit of any kind between them:

| | r1 | r2 | r3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| before the restart | 36 | 195 | 100 |
| **fresh server** | **156** | **100** | **138** |

The spread does not collapse. It should not have been expected to here, and the
reason is worth stating so nobody re-runs this experiment: **no file the dev
server serves was edited at any point in this session.** Everything I wrote
lives in `tools/map-qa`, which node runs directly and Vite never sees, so the
server had never hot-reloaded anything. The other worker's finding is not
contradicted — it is simply not reachable from these runs.

## 3. What the spread actually is: a few reproducible modes, not noise

This is the useful part, and it fell out of the restart.

Fresh r1 reproduced an earlier run **exactly**: `transport@1,8` 93 frames,
`transport@0,8` 76, `transport@1,7` 35, worst depth .283 — identical to the
digit to the pre-restart r2. Fresh r2 reproduced a different earlier run
exactly: 100 frames, `road@3,10` 30, `road@4,10` 27, `road@-9,-11` 20,
`road@6,-6` 13 — identical to the pre-restart r3, across a server restart.

So a run is not a draw from a continuous distribution. **It lands in one of a
handful of discrete, bit-for-bit reproducible worlds**, and which one it lands
in is decided before the synthetic clock takes over — by how many real frames
elapse while the district builds, which is the one thing `lib.mjs` documents
itself as unable to pin. That is why the historical record keeps repeating the
same figures (141 twice; my own 100 twice).

Two consequences, and the first is the most valuable thing in this file:

- **This metric can be made sharp, cheaply.** Pin the district build to a fixed
  number of real frames — or settle the crowd under the synthetic clock before
  arming the measurement — and the modes collapse to one. `lib.mjs`'s
  `region()` already ticks a 600-frame warmup *after* capture is armed; the
  uncontrolled part is the real frames between the region click and the scene
  publishing itself. Fixing that turns a metric nobody can read into one that
  three replicates settle. **I would do this before any further map work of any
  kind**, and it is in the harness, not in anybody's scene files.
- Until then, compare arms **mode by mode**, not by average. Two runs that
  produce the same site list are the same world and can be differenced
  meaningfully; two runs 36 and 195 apart are different worlds and cannot.

I withdraw the "ten to twenty replicates per arm" advice from the section
above in favour of this: the replicates are not the problem, the unpinned start
is, and it is fixable.

## 4. Which of the earlier conclusions still stand

- **The classification result stands.** 121 of 528 contacts involving a
  crossing at all, 280 being a tram against somebody on a pavement, is a
  proportion computed *within* each run, and it held across three runs that
  landed in different modes (187 / 291 / 50 totals). Mode changes the size of
  the number, not what the contacts are made of.
- **The tram-overlap geometry result stands** — it is frameless, and it
  reproduced to the vertex.
- **"The instrument is a coincidence counter"** is half right and needs the
  correction above: it counts coincidences, but the coincidences are
  reproducible, so it is a mode selector rather than a random variable.
- **"The Arc is the outlier"** is now doubtful. On these numbers the Arc is
  worse by roughly an order of magnitude, but The Circuit is not zero, and the
  gap between them is smaller than one district's mode spread.

## 5. What separates the Arc, as far as I got

Measured with `tools/map-qa/track-exposure.mjs` (new), which splits the two
factors a contact needs — somebody standing in the swept path, and a vehicle
arriving while they are there:

| district | walker-frames on the tracks | share | tram traversals per run | tram hull |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Old Quarter | 332, 332, 333 | .0062 | 0.79 (one tram) | hx 2.525, hz 0.36 |
| The Circuit | 4 | .0002 | 0.79 + 0.83 (two) | same |
| Sovereign Arc | 0, 0, 129 | .0000–.0033 | 0.79 + 0.83 (two) | same |

**Service frequency is dead as an explanation**: every district runs the same
carriage model, on a line of the same length (32.1 to 32.2), at the same rate.
The Arc and The Circuit both run two.

**Occupancy is dead as an explanation too, and in the wrong direction**: the
Old Quarter has *twenty times* the Arc's time-on-the-tracks, deterministically
— 332, 332, 333, all of it one walker on way 646 at `(-17,8)` — and it is the
district with three zeros.

So both candidates I named are refused by measurement. What is left, and what I
would look at next, is **phase**: the Old Quarter's occupied site is occupied
for 9% of the run by a walker who is *always there*, and its single tram passes
0.79 times, so the coincidence either happens or does not depending on where
the tram starts. The Arc's occupancy is small but its two trams give it twice
the chances. That is a coin-flip model, and it predicts exactly the mode
structure in §3 — which is the same finding arriving from the other side.

If that is right, the Arc is not different in kind from the Old Quarter at all;
it is the same defect with a slightly worse dice roll, and the fix for both is
the walker standing on the tracks rather than anything in the crossing rules.
Confirming it needs the pinned start from §3, then a mode-by-mode comparison.

Two verification notes on the exposure probe, so it is not over-read: the
trams sit exactly on the curve it masks (`worstOffCurve` 0.000 on every path in
every district, checked because a mask built on the wrong line would report an
honest-looking zero), and the carriage hull the contact test uses is
`hx 2.525, hz 0.36` centred 1.75 m behind its origin — a real 5 m carriage, not
a bounding box around a whole rake, so the contacts are contacts.

## 6. The Circuit's defect, named exactly, and it is a crossing fault

Classified with `crossing-attrib.mjs`, three replicates, 3600 frames:

| replicate | total hits | crossing, at kerb | crossing, moving | pavement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| r1 | 0 | — | — | — |
| r2 | 58 | 55 | 3 | 0 |
| r3 | 103 | 56 | 5 | 42 |

Unlike the Arc, **The Circuit's residue is a crossing fault, and it is one
walker at one kerb**: `(0.57,-9.35)`, reported as `road@4,-5`'s neighbour site
`1,-9`, in both runs that have any contact at all.

The event dump says what happens, and it is not a mistimed step-off:

```
frame 2003  at [0.57,-9.35]  progress 0  phase wait  depth 0.013
frame 2004  at [0.57,-9.35]  progress 0  phase wait  depth 0.033
...
frame 2014  at [0.57,-9.35]  progress 0  phase wait  depth 0.202
```

The walker is **stationary at the kerb with `crossProgress` 0**, and the depth
climbs monotonically to .21 against a .2835 body. Nothing about the walker
moves. **The car drives into a pedestrian who is standing still, waiting.**
The same shape repeats in r3 at frame 2487.

The crossing itself is well formed: length 1.14 m, two conflicts on edges 86
and 88, `kerbside` false, `noConflicts` false. So the link knows about the
road, and the walker is correctly refusing to step off. It is being hit where
it stands.

Two candidate mechanisms, both in `map-agents.ts`, both mine, neither tested:

1. **The kerb it waits at is inside the carriageway.** `crossProgress` 0 puts
   the body exactly on the link's first endpoint, which is a pavement end, and
   the pavement ends on this map are documented as sometimes beginning between
   the kerbs. A walker waiting *on* the endpoint is then waiting in the road.
   If so the repair is to hold the waiting body a body-radius back along its
   own pavement rather than on the link's start, which is small and local.
2. **`claimRoadway` deliberately declines to claim while waiting** when a
   vehicle is already at the conflict point — the deadlock-breaker at the
   `timeToConflict(conflict, .05) <= 0` line. That is right for a walker on a
   safe kerb and wrong for one whose kerb is in the lane, because it hands the
   lane to a driver who then has no reason to avoid the body standing in it.
   Each conflict here reports exactly 24 approaches, which is `APPROACH_CAP`,
   so the approach list is truncated and may also be missing the arm the car
   actually came down; worth checking before assuming the claim is the issue.

**I did not attempt either.** Both change kerb behaviour in every district, the
metric cannot presently separate a real improvement from a mode change (§3),
and the honest sequence is to pin the district build first. But this is the
most tractable thing anybody has found in this area: one site, one walker, a
reproducible signature, a monotonic depth trace, and two named suspects.

## 7. The kerb is in the road, and that is why the car hits her

§6 named the defect and two suspects. The first one is right, and it is not
quite what it looked like.

**The kerb is not inside the carriageway. It is inside the traffic.** Those are
different things on this map, and the difference is authored deliberately.
`TrafficSim`'s constructor solves a lane offset per edge, and where a two-way
street is too narrow to hold two bodies abreast it prefers an overhang past the
kerb to a head-on — its own comment says so, and says the real fault is a
carriageway drawn narrower than the traffic assigned to it. So a pavement end
can be correctly outside the carriageway and still inside the path of the cars
using it.

Measured as pure geometry, with `kerb-audit.mjs`, against the lane centres the
sim actually drives rather than the carriageway. `standing` is the gap between
a waiting walker's own disc and the side of a passing vehicle:

| district | kerbs a walker waits at | with a body in the traffic | worst `standing` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Circuit | 51 | 31 (20 of them timed) | −.244 |
| Old Quarter | 814 | 5 | −.021 |
| Sovereign Arc | 155 | 140 (36 timed) | −.469 |

That is the district ordering, and it is not noisy: The Circuit's waiting
bodies overlap the traffic by a quarter of a metre, the Old Quarter's by two
centimetres. **The Old Quarter scores zero because its kerbs are genuinely
clear**, not because its crossings are timed better.

### The fix, and how it was measured

`resolveStandoffs` steps each waiting position out of the swept path at build
time: back down the pavement first, which is what a pedestrian would do, and
straight out of the offending lane where backing along the link would stay in
it. A junction mouth is typically inside the *other* street's lane, and the
first candidate alone left the largest site untouched — 805 exposed frames fell
only to 519 until the second direction was added, then to 217.

Refused where the step would end in a building. That is why The Circuit still
has ~125 exposed frames at (0.57,−1.38): a .58-wide two-way alley whose two
lanes need .53 either side, with terraces immediately behind both pavements.
There is nowhere on that street to stand that is not in the traffic. **That one
is for the plan, not for the crowd** — it is exactly the case the lane solver's
comment describes.

New probe, `wait-exposure.mjs`: wait-frames, and how many of them are spent
with the body inside a swept path. This is the right instrument for this defect
because the *standing* is deterministic where the *contact* is a coincidence —
a car has to happen to come — and it is the coincidence that gives
`bodyInVehicleFrames` its mode structure. Arm and control in one server
lifetime, standoff switched at runtime with `MAPS_STANDOFF=off`:

| district | exposed wait-frames, off | on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Circuit | 805 / 1136 (70.9%) | 217 / 1560 (13.9%) |
| Old Quarter | 138 / 3697 (3.7%) | 69 / 4151 (1.7%) |
| Sovereign Arc | 111 / 1081 (10.3%) | 29 / 1244 (2.3%) |

**The named site is gone.** (0.57,−9.35), the walker in §6's event trace, holds
55 exposed frames in the control and none in the arm.

### Killed: widening the conflict search to the swept path

The same confusion of carriageway with traffic appears on the other side of the
crossing. `resolveConflicts` builds its conflict list at `edge.width / 2`, so a
crossing does not know about traffic overhanging a kerb: it neither waits for
those cars nor claims their lane. Classified, The Circuit's residue after the
standoff was exactly that — a car at full speed on an edge that was not in the
walker's conflict list, .23 deep.

Taking the reach as `max(carriageway, laneOffset + halfVehicle)` fixes that by
construction, and it was measured with the extra conflicts tagged so they could
be stripped at runtime and compared in one lifetime. It costs the Old Quarter:

| arm | The Circuit | Old Quarter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| carriageway | 0, 0, 0 | 0, 0, 0 |
| swept | 0, 0 | 104, 3 |

104 frames at `road@25,-9` against three zeros, on the district that must not
regress, and no demonstrated benefit — The Circuit read zero in that lifetime
either way. **Reverted.** The likely mechanism is worth recording: the extra
conflicts make more links timed crossings, more cars hold at them, and a car
held *on* a crossing line is something the walker then walks through. The
residue that remains after this revert is still real; it just needs the walker
to not walk into a stopped car, which is a different piece of work.

### Killed: holding the crowd to the clear part of each pavement

`road@4,-5`, the site the collision probe reports on The Circuit, is *not* the
walker of §6. `collide.mjs` keys its sites by the **vehicle's** position, and
the two were conflated: the waiting walker at (0.57,−9.35) and `road@4,-5` are
different events. Classified, `road@4,-5` is a walker on a pavement, not at a
kerb — the same overhang met by somebody walking past rather than standing.

So the band was narrowed to the part of each pavement out of the swept path,
clamped after every other lateral decision, which cannot move anyone outside a
band already cut to be walkable. It does nothing:

- **It narrowed no way at all on The Circuit.** Intersecting the clear offsets
  along a whole way is too strict — one vertex where the entire band is in a
  lane condemns the way — and the district's pavements have such a vertex. A
  per-station band, as `cutFootwaysAroundSolids` already keeps for solids,
  would be needed.
- **The measure it was aimed at does not discriminate.** Walker-frames inside a
  swept path: 33% on The Circuit, **39% on the Old Quarter**, 31% on the Arc.
  The district with no contacts has the most. Narrowing moved it 39→36% on the
  Old Quarter and 31→34% on the Arc, both inside the run-to-run spread.

**Reverted.** The lesson is the useful part, and it is why the standoff works
where this does not: being inside a lane is normal on these pavements and is
not by itself a collision. What makes a collision is *standing still* in one
for seconds while the traffic comes round. `wait-exposure.mjs` keeps both
figures so the next worker can see the difference rather than rediscover it.

### Still open, in order

1. **A walker mid-crossing walks through a stationary vehicle.** Seen at
   `13,-5` on The Circuit, depth .24, the vehicle at `speed: 0` on the conflict
   edge. `gapIsSafe` is consulted only at step-off, so nothing handles a car
   that arrives and stops in front of a walker already crossing. The obvious
   fix — hold the walker — deadlocks against `markPedestrian`, which is why
   `claimRoadway` already drops the claim in the analogous kerb case. Needs a
   mid-crossing equivalent of that rule.
2. **`gapIsSafe` accepting a gap that is not there.** In one control run a
   walker stepped off in front of a car .35 m away doing 2.17 m/s. `timeAlong`
   has been fixed for this three times and the comment at its `approach < -.1`
   branch records two reverts; the remaining hole is most likely the one-frame
   edge-transition described above `refile`, which re-files from last frame's
   positions and so cannot see an agent that changed edge during its own
   update. Do not reopen this without a probe that can see the transition
   frame.
3. **`road@4,-5` on The Circuit**, a walker on a pavement clipped by a car. The
   band idea above is the obvious attack and needs a per-station band to work.
4. **The .58-wide alley on The Circuit**, above. Plan-side, as are the
   pavements that put a third of every district inside a lane.

## 8. Sovereign Arc, site by site: it is one pavement, and it is not a crossing

| class | hits | depth | sites |

| `pavement/transport` | **204** | .271 | (1,7) ×199, (2,7) ×5 |
| `crossing-go/road` | 2 | .084 | (−10,−11) |
| `crossing-wait/road` | 2 | .060 | (−10,−11) |
| `pavement/road` | 1 | .130 | (−10,−11) |

**The Arc's number is one place and it is the tram.** 204 of 209 hits are
walkers simply on a pavement while the tram passes through them, at a single
site, on a way that is not flagged obstructed. Five hits in the whole run
involve a crossing or a car. Nothing a crossing rule does can touch this, which
is why the standoff moved the Arc's frames not at all (175/30 against 212/30)
while cutting its exposed *wait* frames from 12.9% to 1.2%.

Named exactly, from `rail-overlap.mjs`: **way 67, street 8**, whose vertices at
(0.74, 7.39), (1.15, 7.38), (2.43, 7.23) and (3.54, 6.86) sit .09 to .32 from
the tram centreline against a .61 half-beam. The pavement does not cross the
alignment, **it runs down it** for about 2.8 m.

That last part is the distinction §5 missed, and it is the same lesson as the
standoff. Overlap *count* does not separate the districts — the Old Quarter has
43 overlapping vertices across 31 ways against the Arc's 36 across 11 — and
neither does overlap *depth*: the Old Quarter's worst is −.735, deeper than the
Arc's −.746 is by a hair. What differs is that the Old Quarter's are almost all
single vertices where a pavement crosses the track square, which a walker is
through in a stride, while the Arc has a pavement laid along it. Dwell, not
overlap. (The Old Quarter does have one short run of the same kind, way 635 on
street 26, and scores zero, so this is a plausible reading of one site rather
than a law.)

**This belongs to the plan, not to the crowd.** A pavement drawn along a tram
alignment is a siting fault, and the crowd's only options — refusing to spawn
there, or holding walkers off one side of the band — are both papering over it,
the second having already been measured as useless (§7). Routing this to
whoever owns the Arc's plan is worth more than anything the crowd can do to it.

## Harness notes for the next worker on this machine

- **There is no Playwright Chromium on this machine.** `lib.mjs` defaults to
  `chromium-1234` under `~/Library/Caches/ms-playwright`, and that directory
  does not exist at all. The system Chrome works and is what every number here
  was taken with:
  `MAPS_CHROME="/Applications/Google Chrome/Google Chrome.app/Contents/MacOS/Google Chrome"`.
  Do not spend a run on this; it presents as a browser download failure.
- **The API on 5001 is dead** — it accepts a connection and answers nothing, so
  the login page renders without its dev-login button and the harness fails
  after a 180-second timeout that looks like a missing selector. The backend on
  **5091** works. Start Vite with `LSAT_API_PORT=5091`.
- **Two node versions.** `/bin/zsh` resolves `node` to Volta's 18.17.0, which
  Playwright refuses; the interactive path resolves to Homebrew's 26.5.0. Any
  script must name `/opt/homebrew/bin/node` outright.
- **A backgrounded dev server is killed when its parent shell call returns**,
  `nohup` and `disown` included. It has to be started as a tracked background
  job or it will be gone by the next command, presenting as
  `ERR_CONNECTION_REFUSED` from `page.goto`.
- Runs cost about 20-25 s for `crossing-attrib.mjs` and about 20 s for
  `collide.mjs` at 3600 frames on this machine, both single-region. That is
  much cheaper than earlier sessions assumed and it is what makes ten
  replicates reasonable.
- **The stack I measured on is shared.** Vite on 5573 from this worktree, but
  the backend on 5091 belongs to `/private/tmp/lsat-mapgen` and holds the demo
  account. Every arm here saw the same account, so the comparisons are sound,
  but these absolute figures are not comparable with a run against a different
  account, exactly as the session-four notes warn.
