# Sovereign Arc: the remaining walker-versus-vehicle contacts

Worktree `/private/tmp/lsat-crossing`, branch `feat/crossing`, cut from
`integration/all-features` at `7977772`. Session ran 20:12 to 21:40.

**Nothing in `frontend/` was changed. There is nothing to revert.** The two
commits on this branch are both QA probes. What follows is a measurement
result, and its headline is that the premise this session was given is wrong.

## The premise, and why it does not survive measurement

The brief inherited this conclusion from `.map-generator-notes.md`:

> Sovereign Arc's remaining sites are all pavement at a crossing — `road@0,-19`,
> `road@0,15`, `road@9,9`, `road@0,14` — which is a crossing-timing question and
> a different defect.

That was inferred from **site coordinates**, and a coordinate cannot tell apart
the two things that produce one:

- a walker halfway across a road when a car arrives — a timing fault, fixable in
  the crossing rules, which is what this session was scoped to;
- a walker on a pavement that lies inside a carriageway or a tram alignment,
  who is not crossing anything and whose timing cannot be improved because no
  timing decision is being taken.

`tools/map-qa/crossing-attrib.mjs` (new, commit `c9df6be`, corrected in
`3b75c2c`) asks the walker instead of the map: at the moment of containment it
reads `walker.crossing`, `crossPhase`, `crossProgress`, the conflict list the
crossing was built with, and the edge the vehicle was actually on. Same
containment test as `collide.mjs` — shoulder disc against the hull's oriented
rectangle, no margin — and the same body radius, read off a live rig by the
same arithmetic.

### Sovereign Arc, 3600 frames, three replicates, every contact classified

| replicate | total hits | crossing, moving | crossing, at kerb | pavement, road | pavement, tram |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| r1 | 187 | 23 | 17 | 73 | 74 |
| r2 | 291 | 12 | 45 | 28 | 206 |
| r3 | 50 | 21 | 3 | 26 | 0 |
| **total** | **528** | **56** | **65** | **127** | **280** |

**Twenty-three per cent of the contacts involve a walker who is crossing at
all, and about half of that is a walker still standing at the kerb** — depths
of .004 to .066 against a .2835 body, which is a graze by a passing flank, not
a body in a vehicle. The largest single class, at 53%, is a walker on a
pavement being run down by a tram.

The flags rule out the failure modes the previous fixes addressed: `kerbside`
0, `noConflicts` 0, `pastEnd` 0 on every crossing-state hit across all three
runs. So the untimed-crossing fix and the hold-until-the-body-arrives fix are
both still doing their job — every crossing-state contact happened on a link
that *had* a resolved conflict list and was being timed against it.

## The instrument is even noisier than the brief says, and here is the spread

`collide.mjs`, `MAPS_FRAMES=3600`, Sovereign Arc, `inVehicle.bodyInsideFrames`,
three replicates on this stack, no change of any kind between them:

| r1 | r2 | r3 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **36** | **195** | **100** |

`pedestrianHits` on the same three runs: 136 / 561 / 62. The worst-site lists
have **no site in common** between replicates:

- r1 `road@-9,-11` 21, `road@6,-6` 13, `road@7,-6` 2
- r2 `transport@1,8` 93, `transport@0,8` 76, `transport@1,7` 35, `road@6,-5` 33
- r3 `road@3,10` 30, `road@4,10` 27, `road@-9,-11` 20, `road@6,-6` 13

This is consistent with the historical 51 / 66 / 141 / 141 / 187 / 227 and with
the 127 in the brief. **The figure is not a rate, it is a coincidence counter.**
One walker who happens to be standing on the tram alignment when the tram comes
round contributes ninety-odd frames on its own; the same scene with the crowd a
few centimetres out of phase contributes none. The mechanism is visible in the
early replicates I took at the wrong body radius, where five of seven runs
scored exactly zero and the other two scored 80 and 23.

**Consequence for anybody who follows.** To detect a change of the size on
offer here — say 127 down to 60 — against a spread of 36 to 195, you need
something like fifteen to twenty replicates per arm, not three. At roughly 25
seconds a run that is affordable; at three replicates it is not, and three
replicates is what everybody, including this session, has been budgeting. I
would not accept any result on this metric under ten replicates per arm.

The probe is reading the scene, not failing open: `walkerFrames` is 39578 to
39587 on every run, and `liveVehicleFrames` and `vehicles` (17) are stable, so
a zero is a real zero.

## The tram overlap: a hypothesis raised and then killed in the same hour

Since the dominant class is a walker on a pavement inside a tram's path, the
obvious next question is whether pavement is laid inside the tram alignment.
`planFootways` cuts pavements against carriageways only and says so — "a rail
line or a shipping lane crossing a pavement is not a kerb" — and the railway
right-of-way is cut separately by the scene.

`tools/map-qa/rail-overlap.mjs` (new) answers it as pure geometry, no frames,
so it is free of the burstiness above: for every footway vertex, is the walkable
band, `|centre| + halfWidth`, inside the transport's swept half-beam?

| district | ways | overlapping vertices | ways affected | body-inside-vehicle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sovereign Arc | 165 | 36 | 11 | 36 / 195 / 100 |
| Old Quarter | 804 | 43 | 31 | **0** |

**The Old Quarter has more of this defect than the Arc and scores zero.** So
pavement-inside-the-tram-path does not by itself produce contacts, and a pass
that cleaned it up could not be justified by these numbers. Whatever separates
the two districts is something else — tram frequency over the shared spans, or
which pavements the crowd actually populates — and I did not get to it.

**Caveat on this probe, stated because it changes what the number means.** It
counts vertices near the path and does not distinguish a pavement that *runs
along* the alignment from one that legitimately *crosses* it at a right angle;
a level crossing puts a vertex near the line too. The counts are therefore an
upper bound on the real defect, and the Arc's worst entries — way 67 at
`(0.74,7.39)`, `(1.15,7.38)`, `(2.43,7.23)`, and way 13 at `(-0.74,7.72)`, all
with the band .45 to .75 m inside the swept path — want eyes on them before
anybody acts. Note that those coordinates are exactly the `transport@1,7`,
`transport@0,8` and `transport@1,8` sites r2 named.

## What I would do next, in order

1. **Re-baseline with ten or more replicates before touching anything.** Every
   conclusion drawn on this metric from three runs, including the "127", is a
   draw from a distribution whose spread is wider than any fix being proposed.
   `tools/map-qa/run-attrib.sh <arm> <n> continent` does this in about 25
   seconds a run and prints the classification, so ten replicates is four
   minutes.
2. **Split the two districts' tram behaviour**, since geometry alone does not
   explain the gap. The question is why the Old Quarter's 43 overlapping
   vertices never produce a contact and the Arc's 36 produce most of one. If
   the answer is that the Arc's tram runs a shuttle over a populated pavement
   and the Old Quarter's does not, the fix is in the alignment, not in timing.
3. **Only then consider the crossing rules.** They are responsible for at most
   a quarter of this number and about an eighth of it at any depth worth
   calling a containment. Even a perfect crossing fix leaves three quarters of
   the contacts standing, which is the single most useful thing in this file.

## What I did not do, and why

- **No change to `map-agents.ts`.** I own it and I did not touch it. Every
  candidate I could see — widening `APPROACH_REACH`, loosening the
  `claimRoadway` window, treating rail edges as conflicts — targets the 23%,
  and none of them could have been validated against a 36-to-195 spread in the
  time available. Landing an unmeasured change to the crossing rules on a
  branch whose other two districts are at zero is exactly the trade the brief
  says not to make.
- **The other two districts were not re-measured on this branch.** They did not
  need to be: nothing in `frontend/` changed, so their zeros stand where the
  merge left them. If anybody lands a scene change here, they need re-checking.
- **The `(6,-6)` crossing-state failure is not diagnosed.** It is the one
  genuine timing site, it recurs across replicates (13 frames in r1 and r3 of
  the `collide.mjs` set, depth .071), and it is small. The event dump in
  `crossing-attrib.mjs` records the vehicle's edge against the conflict's
  approach list, which is what would say whether the car came down an arm the
  crossing never watched; the run that captured it happened before I added the
  dump, and the runs after it did not reproduce it. Ten replicates would.

## Harness notes for the next worker on this machine

- **There is no Playwright Chromium on this machine.** `lib.mjs` defaults to
  `chromium-1234` under `~/Library/Caches/ms-playwright`, and that directory
  does not exist at all. The system Chrome works and is what every number here
  was taken with:
  `MAPS_CHROME="/Applications/Google Chrome/Google Chrome.app/Contents/MacOS/Google Chrome"`.
  Do not spend a run on this; it presents as a browser download failure.
- **The API on 5001 is dead** — it accepts a connection and answers nothing, so
  the login page renders without its dev-login button and the harness fails
  after a 180-second timeout that looks like a missing selector. The backend on
  **5091** works. Start Vite with `LSAT_API_PORT=5091`.
- **Two node versions.** `/bin/zsh` resolves `node` to Volta's 18.17.0, which
  Playwright refuses; the interactive path resolves to Homebrew's 26.5.0. Any
  script must name `/opt/homebrew/bin/node` outright.
- **A backgrounded dev server is killed when its parent shell call returns**,
  `nohup` and `disown` included. It has to be started as a tracked background
  job or it will be gone by the next command, presenting as
  `ERR_CONNECTION_REFUSED` from `page.goto`.
- Runs cost about 20-25 s for `crossing-attrib.mjs` and about 20 s for
  `collide.mjs` at 3600 frames on this machine, both single-region. That is
  much cheaper than earlier sessions assumed and it is what makes ten
  replicates reasonable.
- **The stack I measured on is shared.** Vite on 5573 from this worktree, but
  the backend on 5091 belongs to `/private/tmp/lsat-mapgen` and holds the demo
  account. Every arm here saw the same account, so the comparisons are sound,
  but these absolute figures are not comparable with a run against a different
  account, exactly as the session-four notes warn.
