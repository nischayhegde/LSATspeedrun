## Part B — The cases confusion, answered plainly

### The concepts that actually exist

| Concept | Origin | How acquired | Pays | Where in UI | How it ends |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A case** | One `StudySessionItem` = **one LSAT question** | Generated on demand, infinite supply | `base_fee × score_mult × firm_mult` + streak + staff + contract bonus (`game.py:2696-2721`) | Case view | You answer it |
| **A practice run** | `StudySession`, `mode='practice'` | "Start 10 cases" on Practice | n/a — the 10 cases inside it pay | Practice → Cases tab | 10 answered, or Discard |
| **The run queue** | Up to 8 unfinished `StudySession`s | Side effect of starting runs | n/a | Practice → run queue list | Finish / pause / discard |
| **Client contract** | `PlayerClientContract` | Firm → Clients → "Work for X" | Sets the `base_fee` used by every case | Firm → Clients | **Never.** Auto-renews at zero (`game.py:2716-2718`) |
| **Pro bono** | A `matter_type` flag on 10 clients | Same button as any other client | `PRO_BONO_FEE_SHARE = .55` of market rate, plus reputation bonuses and loss caps | Firm → Clients, with a seal | Same as any contract |
| **Walk-in client** | `CLIENTS[0]`, `key="walk_in"`, $100 | The tier-0 default; also the fallback when your client is on hold | $100 base fee | Firm → Clients; named in the Practice header | Same as any contract |
| **Mega-litigation** | `StudySession`, `mode='diagnostic'` | Practice → Mega-litigation tab, behind a gate | Sets projected score; >70% promotes a tier | Practice → Mega tab | 75 questions in one timed sitting |
| **Review / repair queue** | FSRS due cards | **Not acquired** — silently folded into each 10-case run (`repairsIncluded`) | Same as any case | A line of caption text on Practice | Answered inside the run |
| **Story quest** | `StoryQuest` | **Office popup**, "Take the matter" (`office-event.tsx:92`) | `quest_bonus` on top of case payouts | Office page, modal | Objective met over N cases |

### The plain answer

**There is no such thing as "receiving a case" in this game, and there is no such thing as
"accepting a case" either. Cases are minted on demand, infinitely, by one button on the Practice
tab.** Everything else the player reads as case-acquisition is something else wearing case-shaped
language.

Specifically, the two things the user cannot tell apart are not two ways of getting cases. They are:

* **Firm → Clients is a price list.** Choosing a client does not fetch you any work. It sets the
  per-question fee, and nothing else. The word "contract", the counter "8 cases remain", the button
  "Work for Ada Mercer", the status pill "AVAILABLE", and the aside headed "NEXT CASE FILE" all
  imply an inventory of matters waiting to be taken. There is no such inventory.
* **Practice → "Start 10 cases" is the only place work exists,** and it never mentions that the
  client you picked is what makes those ten questions worth $100 or $72,000,000 each.

So they are **not** duplicated mechanics. They are one mechanic (choose your rate) and one mechanic
(do the work), described in vocabulary that makes them look like two competing ways of getting
cases. The fix is not explanation, it is deleting the fictional inventory:

1. `cases_remaining` is **not a supply of work.** It is a progress bar to a bonus payment, and it
   refills itself forever. Calling it "cases remaining in this contract" is the single most
   misleading string in the app.
2. The Firm → Clients panel duplicates the Practice header. Both render the same client, the same
   base fee, the same remaining count, under three different names — `active_client`,
   "CURRENT WORKING CLIENT", and `effectiveClient()`/"EFFECTIVE CLIENT".
3. The "OPEN CASE FILE / NEXT CASE FILE" aside (`firm-page.tsx:239-245`) exists *purely* to explain
   the confusion this vocabulary creates. It costs an extra network round-trip
   (`currentCaseQuery`, line 86) to render an explanation of a problem that should not exist.

**Recommendation: delete the fictional inventory, name the thing what it is (a retainer that sets
your rate), and state the rate once.**

## Part C — Connections

### What they are
`game.py:360-391`, list `CONNECTIONS`, 14 entries from `local_bar` ($4,500) to `interworld_assembly`
($26B), folded into `ASSETS` at line 453.

> **Correction.** My first pass read the *authored* literals in `game.py` and reported that
> connections carry no effects and that their clients were dominated by 17 of 21. Both figures were
> wrong: `_rebalance_asset_catalog()` and `_rebalance_client_catalog()` run at import (`game.py:959`)
> and **overwrite every cost, payout and fee in the file**. The authored numbers are inputs to a
> pricing model, not the values the game uses. Everything below is measured after import, by
> importing `app.game` and reading the live catalog. The conclusion got stronger, not weaker.
>
> This also kills the obvious fix: hand-editing client fees in the source is a literal no-op,
> because the rebalancer overwrites them unconditionally on the next import.

### They are not decorative — they are a real unlock gate, and they do carry a payout
Post-rebalance, every connection carries a `payout_mult` of +2% to +6% (assigned by
`_rebalance_asset_catalog`, `game.py:907-911`), and all 14 gate at least one client through
`_client_is_unlocked` (`game.py:1590`). So the suspicion that nothing reads them is wrong on both
counts. The problem is not that they do nothing. It is that everything they do is done better and
cheaper by something else.

They are also visible outside the Firm tab: all 14 have `OFFICE_ASSET_MANIFEST` entries in zone
`relationship-wall`, and `office-three.tsx:1947-1956` builds a real seal per owned connection on the
chambers floor, with focus targets attached.

### Finding 1: the unlock cannot pay, because the client catalog is deliberately value-flat
`_rebalance_client_catalog` (`game.py:942`) sets

```
base_fee = case_target(tier) * share / average_value_factor
```

where `average_value_factor` folds in the client's own `payout_mult`, `contract_bonus_mult` and
contract `length`. It is an equaliser: a client with stronger modifiers is given a *lower* base fee
so that expected value per case comes out at `case_target(tier)` for everyone, times `share`
(1, or `PRO_BONO_FEE_SHARE = .55` for pro bono). Measured spread of base fees within a tier is only
1.06×–1.42×, and the variation runs inverse to the modifiers, which is the equaliser working.

That is a good design — it makes a client a play-style choice rather than an income choice — but it
means **"unlocks the corporate clients" cannot make anyone richer, ever.** The one benefit unique to
connections is worth zero by construction.

### Finding 2: on the axis that is left, connections lose to upgrades at every single tier
What remains is the +2%–6% `payout_mult`. Compared against the best upgrade at the same tier:

## Clients: the axis, and why this one

**Chosen axis: when the money arrives — per-case fee against contract close.** Rejected: anything
touching expected value, because `_rebalance_client_catalog` equalises it by construction and that
equalisation is what makes a client a play-style choice rather than an income choice.

The measurement that drove it: `contract_bonus_mult` was 0 for 58 of 69 clients, and the 13 with a
`payout_mult` premium are compensated by a proportionally lower `base_fee`, so that premium is the
same money under another name. The only real separator was contract length, and its effect decayed
from 16–39% of a tier-0 client's value to under 4% by tier 9 — the choice evaporated exactly as the
player got enough money for it to matter.

Now each client's value is split on a target close-share, spread from the steadiest to the most
speculative client at each tier. It is a genuine risk decision rather than flavour because the close
is **forfeitable**: `cases_remaining` only falls on a decisive win, and a reputation slip puts the
client on hold and bills the walk-in instead, stranding the progress. So a speculative contract is a
bet on holding form — which is exactly what the new streak standing protects. Steady clients are
correct when reputation is fragile, speculative ones when a streak is holding the floor up.

Spread achieved: 11.7–14 points of total value between steadiest and most speculative at every tier
from 3 up (roughly 1 point at tier 9 before). Tiers 0–2 stay compressed at 5.6–8.6 points because the
hardcoded `+2` base contract multiplier already exceeds the target share there and the bonus clamps
at zero. `close_share_bps` publishes the *realised* share, not the target, so the card never prints
an intention it did not achieve.

## The streak: three layers, and the anti-farm argument

| Layer | Advances on | Pays |
|---|---|---|
| `current_streak` | A validated win | Cash bonus, 2%/win capped 20–25% — **unchanged** |
| Working-day streak | **A day on which a case is finished** (was: a day the app was opened) | Licenses the layer below |
| `streak_standing` (new, derived) | Both of the above | A reputation **floor**, 1–5 points on a diminishing ladder |

**Anti-farm.** Practice mints cases on demand without limit, so any per-case reward is farmable per
case. The ladder is therefore not the reward — the day count is. A 500-win marathon on day one is
worth exactly 1.0, the same as three wins, because standing is licensed at one point per consecutive
working day and wall-clock days are the one resource the game cannot mint. Idling does not work
either, because a working day now requires a finished case with a real argument (the same bar
`DailyProgress.cases_completed` uses — thin wins do not count). Full 5.0 needs five consecutive days
*and* a 21-win run.

**Composition with the district retainer floor.** Streak standing is added into the same `standing`
argument as `territory_standing`, so both land under the single existing
`TERRITORY_STANDING_FLOOR_CEILING` (90). They compose by addition under one shared cap instead of
being two ladders racing for the top of the game. The existing test that the last gates (91 pro bono,
94 final headquarters) stay payable in casework alone still holds, and is now asserted against the
combined cap.

**Losing a streak.** It is a floor and never a credit: it stops reputation falling, it never pushes
it up. Breaking a streak therefore removes protection rather than deducting points, and the
subsequent drift is still bounded by `maximum_drop`/`CORRECT_DROP_CAP`. A player who breaks a long
streak on a bad day loses a safety net, not a score.

**No migration.** `daily_streak_current`, `daily_streak_best` and `daily_streak_last_date` already
exist from `0026_daily_activity_streak`, and streak standing is derived rather than stored. The
single head remains `0034_comparative_passages`, confirmed by running `flask db upgrade` from empty
on an isolated database: it reported `0034_comparative_passages (head)`, one head.

## Deliberately not done, round two

- **Client payout floors.** `minimum_score_multiplier` would be the sharpest risk lever, but it is
  *not* an input to `average_value_factor`, so adding it raises true EV without compensation and
  would move the curve. Extending the equaliser to model the miss distribution is a much larger and
  riskier change than the one made.
- **Decor left purely cosmetic.** This is a decision, not an omission. It is the only asset class
  whose entire value is the office view, each piece is already an individually authored prop, and it
  was the one tab that already satisfied "look at the room and see what you bought" before any of

  lacked was a route to its own payoff, which it now has.
- **Camera deep-link to a decor item.** The office has no `?focus=` route parameter and adding one

  on the office rather than on the object.
- **Tiers 0–2 client compression**, explained above; it would require letting the contract multiplier

# Round three — retainers in the tab, and what acting on something feels like

Branch `feat/firmux-pass`, commits `e6bf0a2`, `4bdf37b`, `76213e4`, off `552aaea`. Frontend only:
no backend file, no schema, no economy input changed, so the curve is untouched by construction.

## Retainers: what each surface owns

Round one gave connections a real effect — they gate the district retainer board — but left that
board on the map, so the Firm tab sold a key to a door in another room. The board is now in the tab
as well, and the two are deliberately not the same panel:

| | Map `RetainerBoard` | Firm tab `RetainerLedger` |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | The one region on screen | All five regions, switchable |
| Answers | *Where* is it | *What do I hold, what does it pay, what does the next one cost* |
| Carries | Landmark join, camera flight on signing, owned landmarks lit in the scene | Held count, standing against its cap, the lease before and after relief, per-district standing and lease share |
| Hand-off | "All N of M in the ledger" → `/firm?tab=connections` | Pin on any district with a landmark → `/map?district=…` |

The map's foot used to restate firm-wide standing and relief in the corner of a 3D scene; that is
the ledger's job now, and the foot links to it instead. Arriving from the ledger opens the board on
the right region, marks the row that was asked for, and flies to it once the planner has reported
its directory.

The tab strip stays at six. `connections` keeps its key, so every existing deep link and the map's
`onManage('connections')` still land, but it is labelled **Districts**: a connection's whole effect
is the districts it opens, and the panel is now ordered the way the decision is made — the ledger
first, the networks that gate it underneath.

## Interaction

Everything that can be bought in this tab now says so where the money is, on the thing that changed.

- **The treasury moves.** `useRollup` travels the balance to its new figure and `useDelta` names the
  movement (`−$8,297`). Both already existed for the office ledger; neither was wired here.
- **A signature lands on three figures at once.** Signing Millrace Wharf takes held 6→7, standing
  1.66→2.01 and the lease $2,271→$2,200, all rolling, with the row turning held under a pressed
  seal. That is the whole argument for a retainer, made at the moment it is bought.
- **Confirmations stopped being curtains.** `.just-bought` and `.client-activated-flash` covered the
  entire card — the one thing they were confirming — for 1.8s and 2.2s. Both are now a corner stamp,
  and the client one is seated above the card's own button so the control stays clickable.

### Reduced motion

The global rule in `styles.css` collapses every `animation-duration` to `.01ms !important`. Both old
confirmations ended on `opacity: 0`, so they were invisible from the first frame to exactly the
readers who asked for less motion — the same class of defect as the gesture animation frozen at
t=0. Every animation added here **ends on the state worth seeing** and is withdrawn by a React
timer instead of by the tail of a fade. Verified in Chromium with `reducedMotion: 'reduce'`: 320ms
after a signature the seal is `opacity: 1, scale: 1`, the delta chip is on screen, and the three
figures already read their settled values.

### No new dependency, and no three.js

The request named three.js. The firm route is 43 kB and the `three` chunk alone is 734 kB raw /
189 kB gzipped; putting a 3D flourish on this route would multiply its cost by seventeen on a page
already reported as slow, while another worker is cutting bloat. Everything here is CSS transforms,
opacity and width on fixed-size boxes — nothing that costs layout, and no filter or backdrop-filter,
which is what was removed from this same page to take median scroll frame time from 27ms to 16.7ms.
