## Part A — Ground truth: what each subtab actually does

The Firm tab is `frontend/src/pages/firm-page.tsx` (294 lines). Seven subtabs, declared at line 36-46.
Five of the seven (`upgrades`, `decor`, `staff`, `connections`, `rivals`) are **the same grid**
filtered by `asset.type`; only the type filter differs (`typeMap`, line 129). `clients` and
`achievements` are bespoke.

Every purchase in all five grid tabs fires the identical call: `api.purchase(key)` →
`POST /api/game/assets` → `game.purchase_asset` (`backend/app/game.py:1988`), which debits cash,
writes a `PlayerAsset` row, and ledgers it. What differs between the tabs is entirely which effect
fields the asset dict carries.

## Part B — The cases confusion, answered plainly

### The concepts that actually exist

| Concept | Origin | How acquired | Pays | Where in UI | How it ends |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A case** | One `StudySessionItem` = **one LSAT question** | Generated on demand, infinite supply | `base_fee × score_mult × firm_mult` + streak + staff + contract bonus (`game.py:2696-2721`) | Case view | You answer it |
| **A practice run** | `StudySession`, `mode='practice'` | "Start 10 cases" on Practice | n/a — the 10 cases inside it pay | Practice → Cases tab | 10 answered, or Discard |
| **The run queue** | Up to 8 unfinished `StudySession`s | Side effect of starting runs | n/a | Practice → run queue list | Finish / pause / discard |
| **Client contract** | `PlayerClientContract` | Firm → Clients → "Work for X" | Sets the `base_fee` used by every case | Firm → Clients | **Never.** Auto-renews at zero (`game.py:2716-2718`) |
| **Pro bono** | A `matter_type` flag on 10 clients | Same button as any other client | `PRO_BONO_FEE_SHARE = .55` of market rate, plus reputation bonuses and loss caps | Firm → Clients, with a seal | Same as any contract |
| **Walk-in client** | `CLIENTS[0]`, `key="walk_in"`, $100 | The tier-0 default; also the fallback when your client is on hold | $100 base fee | Firm → Clients; named in the Practice header | Same as any contract |
| **Mega-litigation** | `StudySession`, `mode='diagnostic'` | Practice → Mega-litigation tab, behind a gate | Sets projected score; >70% promotes a tier | Practice → Mega tab | 75 questions in one timed sitting |
| **Review / repair queue** | FSRS due cards | **Not acquired** — silently folded into each 10-case run (`repairsIncluded`) | Same as any case | A line of caption text on Practice | Answered inside the run |
| **Story quest** | `StoryQuest` | **Office popup**, "Take the matter" (`office-event.tsx:92`) | `quest_bonus` on top of case payouts | Office page, modal | Objective met over N cases |

### The plain answer

**There is no such thing as "receiving a case" in this game, and there is no such thing as
"accepting a case" either. Cases are minted on demand, infinitely, by one button on the Practice
tab.** Everything else the player reads as case-acquisition is something else wearing case-shaped
language.

Specifically, the two things the user cannot tell apart are not two ways of getting cases. They are:

* **Firm → Clients is a price list.** Choosing a client does not fetch you any work. It sets the
  per-question fee, and nothing else. The word "contract", the counter "8 cases remain", the button
  "Work for Ada Mercer", the status pill "AVAILABLE", and the aside headed "NEXT CASE FILE" all
  imply an inventory of matters waiting to be taken. There is no such inventory.
* **Practice → "Start 10 cases" is the only place work exists,** and it never mentions that the
  client you picked is what makes those ten questions worth $100 or $72,000,000 each.

So they are **not** duplicated mechanics. They are one mechanic (choose your rate) and one mechanic
(do the work), described in vocabulary that makes them look like two competing ways of getting
cases. The fix is not explanation, it is deleting the fictional inventory:

1. `cases_remaining` is **not a supply of work.** It is a progress bar to a bonus payment, and it
   refills itself forever. Calling it "cases remaining in this contract" is the single most
   misleading string in the app.
2. The Firm → Clients panel duplicates the Practice header. Both render the same client, the same
   base fee, the same remaining count, under three different names — `active_client`,
   "CURRENT WORKING CLIENT", and `effectiveClient()`/"EFFECTIVE CLIENT".
3. The "OPEN CASE FILE / NEXT CASE FILE" aside (`firm-page.tsx:239-245`) exists *purely* to explain
   the confusion this vocabulary creates. It costs an extra network round-trip
   (`currentCaseQuery`, line 86) to render an explanation of a problem that should not exist.

**Recommendation: delete the fictional inventory, name the thing what it is (a retainer that sets
your rate), and state the rate once.**

## Part C — Connections

### What they are
`game.py:360-391`, list `CONNECTIONS`, 14 entries from `local_bar` ($4,500) to `interworld_assembly`
($26B), folded into `ASSETS` at line 453.

### They are not decorative — they are a real unlock gate
Unlike upgrades and staff, no connection carries any effect field the economy reads
(`payout_mult`, `passive_hourly`, `staff_flat`, `storage_hours`, `streak_bonus_cap`,
`contract_bonus_mult`, `reputation_guard`). Their entire function is to appear in a client's
`requires` list, checked by `_client_is_unlocked` (`game.py:1590`). **All 14 gate at least one
client.** So the suspicion that nothing reads them is wrong — `_requirements_met` reads them.

They are also visible outside the Firm tab: all 14 have `OFFICE_ASSET_MANIFEST` entries in zone
`relationship-wall`, and `office-three.tsx:1947-1956` builds a real seal per owned connection on the
chambers floor, with focus targets attached.

### But they are economically dominated, which is worse than decorative
I extracted `CLIENTS` and `CONNECTIONS` from `game.py` and compared every connection-gated client
against the best client at the **same tier that requires no connection at all**.
**17 of 21 connection-gated clients pay less than a client the player already has for free.**

| Connection | Cost | Best client it unlocks | Best FREE client, same tier | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| local_bar | $4.5K | Small business $4.5K | Tenants' organizer $8K | dominated |
| business_network | $18K | Private client $18K | Warehouse survivors $38K | dominated |
| board_network | $140K | Regional corporation $85K | Private detective agency $190K | dominated |
| civic_referral_council | $70K | Transit crash class $155K | Private detective agency $190K | dominated |
| entertainment_circle | $330K | Sports league $2.1M | Reform union president $2.8M | dominated |
| international_network | $2M | Stolen-art alliance $3.1M | Reform union president $2.8M | marginal |
| national_gc_council | $6M | National corporation $7M | Cartel accountant $16M | dominated |
| diplomatic_forum | $24M | Exiled publisher $44M | Sanctions investigator $52M | dominated |
| global_exchange | $105M | Rogue AI lab $220M | Deepfake survivors $180M | worth it |
| sovereign_council | $420M | Central bank $540M | Ghost-fleet owner $760M | dominated |
| innovation_compact | $1.5B | Quantum consortium $1.9B | Autonomous city $2.7B | dominated |
| oceanic_compact | $4.5B | Oceanic consortium $5.8B | (none at T11) | worth it |
| orbital_bar | $11B | Orbital industries $13B | Orbital salvager $15B | dominated |
| interworld_assembly | $26B | Planetary assembly $72B | (none at T14) | worth it |

**Cause.** `CLIENTS` is assembled in three passes. Pass 1 (`game.py:712`) is the institutional
ladder and carries almost every connection gate. Pass 2 (`game.py:744`) is the character-driven
clients, added later — they pay more, they carry all the interesting modifiers, and they almost
never require a connection. The later content silently invalidated the earlier gating. Nobody
noticed because the fees are hand-authored constants scattered across 70 lines.

This is the *same failure the codebase already diagnosed and fixed once*, for pro bono clients. The
comment at `game.py:783-788` says it exactly: a hand-authored figure "silently stops meaning
anything the moment the economy is rescaled", which is why `PRO_BONO_FEE_SHARE` was made a derived
number. The connection-gated clients never got the same treatment.

**Net: a player who buys connections is strictly worse off than one who ignores the subtab and
spends the cash on upgrades.** That is not decoration, it is a trap.

### Connections do not gate tier advancement
`TIER_GATED_ASSET_TYPES = {"upgrade", "staff", "rival"}` (`game.py:454`) excludes `connection`. The
tier banner copy ("every prior upgrade, staff hire, and acquisition") is accurate.

### Neighbouring systems checked for the same defect

**District retainers (`0032_district_retainers`) — NOT defective.** `secure_district`
(`game.py:2017`) writes `PlayerTerritory`. Three separate readers consume it:
`_territory_totals` → `standing` and `rent_relief_bps`; `territory_standing` feeds `_career_floor`
(`game.py:1288-1306`), which is a real reputation floor; and `rent_relief` reduces the daily lease
(`game.py:1375`). It is surfaced on the map by `RetainerBoard`
(`unified-empire-map.tsx:80-160`) with a working purchase button, and owned districts join to map
landmarks (`ownedLandmarkKeys`, line 241) and fly the camera there on purchase (line 323).
This system is properly wired. It is just **not in the Firm tab at all**, which is part of why the
Firm tab feels disconnected.

**Trial calendar (`0033_trial_calendar`) — this migration does not exist.** Migration 0033 is
`0033_merge_blind_review`. `backend/app/trial.py` deliberately added **no schema**; its docstring
says so ("no new schema, no second notion of a deadline"). It is a pure projection over
`users.target_score` / `users.target_test_date` and the existing `scoring.py` estimator, rendered by
`frontend/src/trial-calendar.tsx` on the Practice and Dashboard pages. It is read-only by design and
is not a Firm tab surface, so it is out of scope here. It is informational, not inert-by-accident.

open and is visually indistinguishable between the 14 of them.

## Part E — Plan (ranked by player-perceptible impact per unit of risk)

### Stage 1 — Delete the fictional case inventory (frontend only, no economy risk)
The Clients subtab is a price list wearing the costume of a case queue.
* Drop the `currentCaseQuery` fetch and the "OPEN CASE FILE / NEXT CASE FILE" aside; it exists only
  to explain a confusion that this stage removes, and it costs a network round-trip per visit.
* Re-head the status panel so it states the one thing that is true: this is your rate.
* One name for the relationship, everywhere: "retainer", replacing the three-way
  active/working/effective split.
* Files: `frontend/src/pages/firm-page.tsx`, `frontend/src/pages/shared.tsx`,
  `frontend/src/pages/cases-page.tsx`. ~80 lines net removal.

### Stage 2 — Remove the inert Achievements subtab from the Firm tab
No control, no reader, no consequence — it fails the user's own bar. Fold the trophies onto the
Progress page where the player already reviews their record, and take the Firm tab from seven tabs
to six.
* Files: `frontend/src/pages/firm-page.tsx`, `frontend/src/pages/dashboard-page.tsx`. ~60 lines.

### Stage 3 — Make connections load-bearing by repairing the domination (backend)
Derive connection-gated client fees as a premium over the best ungated client at the same tier,
exactly as `PRO_BONO_FEE_SHARE` already does for pro bono, and for the identical stated reason.
This makes the existing "Unlocks X clients" benefit copy *true* without inventing a new mechanic,
without a migration, and without touching any ungated client's fee — so a player who never buys a
connection sees an identical curve.
* Files: `backend/app/game.py`. ~25 lines.
* Must re-run `backend/scripts/simulate_economy_curve.py` and report the curve.

### Stage 4 — Make connections perceptible (office + map)
Label the relationship wall and put connections on the map region they name.
* Files: `frontend/src/art/office-three.tsx`, `frontend/src/art/unified-empire-map.tsx`.
