# Lawyer Tycoon — original pitch narrative

This narrative follows **`Lawyer Tycoon .pptx` and its embedded speaker notes**.
It is intentionally narrower than earlier versions of the web deck. The original
presentation is the content authority; the web deck keeps its stronger graphics,
transitions, and live-product framing without adding a different pitch.

The audience should leave with five ideas:

1. LSAT preparation takes too long; Lawyer Tycoon aims to cut a typical
   four-month study period in half.
2. Repeated real questions and immediate feedback are more efficient than a
   study plan centered on videos and concept instruction.
3. Lawyer Tycoon combines an answer, a written justification, rubric-based
   feedback, and adaptive question/strategy selection in one loop.
4. The tycoon game is optional. Practice advances the game; the game never
   gates practice.
5. The product is narrower, more directed, and more motivating than a
   conventional LSAT course.

## Content boundary

The on-stage pitch may use the facts and claims present in the original PPTX:

- about four months of study for the average student, with a goal of two months;
- an average SAT Reading score of 610 for LSAT test takers;
- more than 40% of time in popular apps going to live/video instruction;
- targeted questions, immediate reasoning feedback, strategy prompts, mock
  exams, spacing, and interleaving;
- more than ten question-type buckets and fourteen strategies;
- an optional virtual-currency tycoon loop;
- 1.3×–3.7× more out-of-class practice per student across three semester-long
  university studies, as shown on the original evidence slide;
- a progression pace of about two months at twenty correct questions per day.

The pitch does **not** use the later deck’s coaching-course effect sizes, market
price ranges, competitor scorecard, confidence research, AI field experiment,
burnout statistics, practice-completion cohort, or other claims that were not in
the original presentation.

## Speaker script

### 1. Lawyer Tycoon — Nischay

We are Nischay and Alan, students at UT Austin, and this is Lawyer Tycoon — an
LSAT speedrun app. The average student spends about four months studying for the
LSAT. Our goal is to cut that timeline in half.

Delivery: open with the problem and the goal. Do not explain the product yet.

### 2. Spiky POV — Nischay

Our spiky point of view is that watching videos and learning concepts is
inefficient. Practicing test-taking strategies on real questions, then receiving
feedback, is the efficient way to improve. The average LSAT test taker scored
610 on SAT Reading; our view is that these students already have the general
knowledge they need. What they need is practice.

Delivery: land the contrast — passive instruction versus repeated questions and
feedback.

### 3. The goal — Nischay

Popular LSAT apps such as 7Sage and LSAT Lab can put more than forty percent of
study time into live or video instruction on concepts. We redirect that time
into targeted practice and immediate feedback on the student’s reasoning. We
suggest a specific strategy during each question and provide the interface to
apply it. The next questions and strategies adapt to past accuracy. Mock exams
are available when a student can set aside the time, and the game loop is
optional for students who want an extra motivation boost.

Delivery: this is the overview. Keep each feature to one sentence; the next
slides show the details.

### 4. MCQ and justification — Alan

Here is the multiple-choice flow. First, we suggest a relevant test-taking
strategy. Each strategy has a custom interface that asks the student to use it.
Then the student answers the multiple-choice question and writes an explanation
for the choice.

Delivery: let the strategy card, answer choice, and written explanation do the
visual work. Do not read the question aloud.

### 5. Feedback on every question — Alan

After every question, an LLM follows a grading rubric and explains what was
correct or incorrect in the student’s reasoning. It also shows the proper
reasoning for the problem. Students can return to feedback from past questions
in the dashboard.

Delivery: pause on the coaching panel and give the audience time to read it.

### 6. Adaptive questions and strategies — Alan

Questions and strategies are personalized using spacing and interleaving.
Question types a student misses appear more often, and strategies the student
performs better with are prompted more often. The system covers more than ten
question-type buckets and fourteen high-yield strategies. That is how we move
beyond a basic question bank or recommendation list.

Delivery: point to the weak areas and strategy record. Do not enumerate every
type or strategy.

### 7. Upgrade with virtual currency — Alan

The game is optional; the adaptive practice system works without it. In the
game, the student starts as a new attorney and uses virtual currency to expand
the firm and increase its income. Practice is what earns visible progress: the
game never gates the questions; the questions gate the game.

Delivery: let the purchase and office update happen before explaining the
inversion.

### 8. The evidence — Alan

A study across three semester-long university courses isolated virtual currency
from the other game elements. Out-of-class practice rose from 1.3 times to 3.7
times per student. That is why our optional game loop uses currency: its job is
to encourage the student to complete more practice.

Delivery: say “practice,” not “scores.” The study supports participation in
out-of-class practice.

### 9. Your law firm — Alan

Upgrades change the look and feel of the law firm. As the student progresses,
the office becomes more robust. The room is a visible record of how many LSAT
questions the student has answered while using a proper strategy.

Delivery: hold on the office. Do not open menus; the environment is the point.

### 10. Progress to higher-tier firms — Alan

The goal is to earn enough virtual currency to progress to the most expensive
law firm. The game is paced to be completed in about two months if a student
answers twenty questions correctly each day.

Delivery: say the first sentence over the career route. Then stop talking and
let the animated map show the progression.

### 11. Why Lawyer Tycoon — Nischay

Lawyer Tycoon focuses on the work that matters most: practice problems. Removing
a large video and live-instruction layer also reduces our overhead. Instead of
asking students to choose comfortable question types, the system targets the
areas where they struggle. Finally, the optional game loop gives students
another reason to keep answering questions. Our bet is simple: more of the same
study time should go into targeted practice, because that is what can drive
faster improvement.

Delivery: synthesize the four differences once. Do not introduce a new claim in
the close.

### 12. Thanks / questions — Nischay

Thank you for listening. We are happy to take your questions.

Delivery: hold this slide for the full Q&A.
